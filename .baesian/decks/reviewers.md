Today a project has one reviewer, its owner. These five cards decide what it means for a project to have several: who it's built for first, whose a card is, who a card reaches first, what a second person's different answer does, and which cards take two answers.

They're about the goals and the model only. The mechanics (the record's formats, how CI trusts a member's passkey, how someone joins, the privacy policy) come as cards after these, since they depend on them. Nothing is built until these are answered.

What the research found, in short: a request to one named person is answered sooner than one to a group; every framework for decisions names one decider; two independent answers catch more than one, at a cost, and only if neither sees the other's; people differ often, so a second answer needs a rule; and nobody has built or studied any of this for agents.

They're best answered in order: the third builds on the second.
