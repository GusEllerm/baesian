title: Citations of Baesian's own decisions in its output
question: Should the command line stop printing citations of Baesian's own design decisions, such as "(B-4rwyz)" or "(D42; D144)", in what it tells people and agents?
doing: a UX pass over the command line's output, asked for by the user
topic: cli/output/citations
deck: cli-output
level: provisional
touches:
  - user-facing
reverse: cheap
proposal: Drop them from all output
proposal_then: "Messages say what to do without citing why. The citations stay where they're useful: in the code's comments, the vault and the Decision Log, for whoever works on Baesian."
alternative: Keep them, behind a verbose flag
alternative_then: Ordinary output drops them; `--verbose` (a new flag) prints them, for someone who wants to trace a message back to the decision behind it.
crux: Does anyone using Baesian in their own repo need to see which of Baesian's decisions a message rests on?
crux_proposal: No
crux_alternative: Yes, sometimes
crux_fact: The audit found about 33 in ordinary output (init --personal, file, check, login, ci, help, errors). Outside this repo they point nowhere a user can follow, and a citation like B-4rwyz has the same shape as the user's own card IDs, so an agent can take it for one of its cards.
rationale: They're notes for Baesian's builders that leaked into the product. A verbose flag would keep a second wording alive for a reader who barely exists.
