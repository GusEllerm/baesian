alternative_then: a new address, GET /v1/projects/{project}/waiting, counts from the project's own store; the projects page lists your projects at once and asks every one of them in parallel, each request waking that project's relay
crux: Should opening your projects page cost one request however many projects you have, or ask each project afresh for an exact count?
crux_proposal: One request
crux_alternative: Ask each afresh
crux_fact: "Each project's relay already works out the cards waiting for you, for its notifications: open cards and drawn audits, with their levels. The projects page reads your account and its projects in one request to the accounts database, 0.2 to 0.7 seconds. Asking a project wakes its relay, 0.1 to 0.8 seconds each, in parallel. You have 2 projects. A stored count is a new table in the accounts database, added by a migration, and could be out of date if a project's write to it failed, until that project next changes. Once cards go to several people, the counts differ by person, so either way they're kept or asked per account. The privacy policy lists everything the service keeps; a stored count is a new item there, counts and levels only, as a notification already says them."
rationale: The page stays one request however many projects you have, and the counts come with the list, so nothing pops in a moment later. The project that changes is the one that writes, right after it works out what's waiting for its notifications, and the alarm that does that already tries again when something fails.
alternative_rationale: Every count is exact the moment you look, from the same code the desk's queue uses, with no second copy to keep in step and no migration. With a handful of projects the parallel requests cost little, and an address that answers "what's waiting for me here" is also what an inbox shared by several people will need.
wrong_if: a count on the projects page disagrees with what the project's desk shows
check: open each project from the projects page for a week and compare the count with the desk

The count is "waiting for you": what the desk's queue holds and a notification counts, not cards answered or held back. A project's row is written when a card is filed, answered, withdrawn, revised or drawn as an audit, and once when the project first wakes after this ships, so existing projects get theirs.
