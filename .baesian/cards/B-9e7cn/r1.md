question: Should a new member's hard answers count in CI once an owner's passkey vouches for them, or only once their passkey is added to CI's settings by hand?

proposal: Added to CI's settings by hand
proposal_then: "nothing new is trusted: each member's passkey gets a line in CI's pin, as the owner's has now, set by someone with rights over the repo. `baesian ci` prints and sets it. Until then CI fails on that passkey, saying how to fix it."
alternative: An owner's passkey vouches
alternative_then: CI pins only the owners' passkeys. A member's counts when the record holds a file adding them that an owner's passkey proved. Joining needs no change to CI; a new signed file and a new check in `baesian verify` are built.
crux: Is changing CI's settings each time someone joins a price worth paying to trust nothing new?
crux_proposal: "Yes: by hand"
crux_alternative: "No: an owner vouches"
crux_preference: true
crux_fact: "A hard answer counts in CI only if the passkey that proved it is in CI's pin (BAESIAN_TRUST), one line per passkey, which `baesian ci --github` sets. Today a passkey in the record that isn't pinned fails `baesian verify`, with a message saying to add its line, or to find out who added it: the guard against the service, or anyone who gets into it, adding a passkey. By hand, every join and every new passkey needs that command run by someone with rights over the repo's settings, and CI is red until it is. With an owner vouching, a join is one tap of an owner's passkey; what CI trusts then rests on the owners' passkeys and on new checking code, and an owner's stolen passkey could add a member, as well as answer."
rationale: For a few developers, people join a handful of times, and the step is one command the owner has already run once. It adds no format and no code to the part of Baesian that decides what counts, which is the last place to move fast. The chain can come when a team is big enough to feel the friction.
alternative_rationale: "A red CI on a new teammate's first hard answer is the first thing they'd see, and fixing it needs someone with admin rights, who may not be the owner who invited them. Friction on the pin is how pins get dropped: a team that tires of it leaves CI unpinned, which is worse than a chain rooted in passkeys. An owner's passkey already lets more cards past the gate; vouching for a member is the same kind of act."
wrong_if: a member's passkey comes to count in CI without someone with rights over the repo having acted
check: the test that a passkey in the record and not in the pin fails verify still passes, with a member's passkey
serves: only someone with rights over the repo can make a passkey count

Without a pin (a repo that hasn't set BAESIAN_TRUST), a passkey counts when the service signed its key file, as now; this card is about pinned CI, which is what `baesian ci` sets up.
