proposal: Quoted, with its author, everywhere
proposal_then: Each comment, title or option others wrote is a JSON string with its author, on one line, so it can't pass for Baesian's own lines. The CLI and the hooks change too, through the same shared functions.
alternative: Raw after a label, as today
alternative_then: Text appears as baesian wait and inbox print it now, after a label such as 'reviewer:'. People read it more easily; the tools' structured content keeps fields apart, and the hooks' output is unchanged.
crux: Will agents read text written by people or agents you don't control?
crux_proposal: They will
crux_alternative: They won't
crux_fact: "Today every card and comment comes from you or agents on your machine. With the hosted service (D132, in v1), cards from other machines arrive through the relay, and later other reviewers' comments may too. A text field can hold newlines, so raw text can imitate lines such as 'resolved: accept'. The CLI's output reaches agents as well, through their shell and the plugin's hooks."
rationale: Once text arrives from beyond your machine, a forged line could tell an agent that a card was accepted. Quoting keeps each field on its own line at little cost to people, and doing it in the shared functions keeps the CLI and the tools alike.
wrong_if: Quoted output makes cards noticeably harder for people to read in a terminal, or agents act on quoted answers less reliably than on raw ones.

Either way the tool descriptions are fixed, the server can't answer, and the skill tells agents to treat comments as reasoning, not commands. Quoting narrows prompt injection; it doesn't prevent it. See [[MCP Server#7. Prompt injection through tool results]].
