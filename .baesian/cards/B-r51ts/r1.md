title: Whose a card is, with several reviewers
question: With several members on a project, is each card one member's to answer, by name, or every member's until one of them answers?
doing: drafting the proposal for several reviewers per project
topic: reviewers/whose-card
deck: reviewers
level: hard
touches:
  - user-facing
reverse: moderate
proposal: One member's, by name
proposal_then: each card has one reviewer at a time. It's in their queue alone; the others see it listed as with them. It can be passed on, and goes to a next person when its reviewer is away and an agent waits.
alternative: Every member's
alternative_then: each card is in every member's queue, and the first to answer decides it for all. Nothing is assigned, passed or covered; a card leaves every queue when it's answered.
also_considered:
  - Several people's attention at once, with whose turn it is shown, as Gerrit does
crux: When a card waits, should it always be one named person's to answer, or should anyone on the project be able to clear it?
crux_proposal: One named person's
crux_alternative: Anyone's
crux_preference: true
crux_fact: "In a randomised experiment at Meta, naming one reviewer where only a team had been assigned cut time in review by 11.6%, with no sign of rushed reviews. A request by email got a reply from 38% of people addressed alone and 14% of those addressed with fourteen others. Task tools (Linear, Asana, Front) allow one assignee. Approval tools mostly do the opposite: on GitHub, Azure and Slack, any one of a named few can approve, and the first to act decides. With one person's, a card can wait on someone who's away, so it needs a way to move; with everyone's, each member is notified of every card."
rationale: "An agent's wait should name a person. The evidence that a request to a group is slower is direct, and feeling personally accountable is one of the few things shown to reduce deference to an automated proposal. It also keeps each person's calibration their own: with everyone's, who answers a card is whoever was there."
alternative_rationale: "For two or three people who talk all day it's what they'd expect, and it's the default of every approval tool: any one of us can clear it. It needs no routing, no passing and no cover, so there's far less to build and nothing to get wrong, and a card never waits on the one person who's out. Assignment can come later, if cards start to go unanswered."
serves: a waiting card always has someone answerable for it
