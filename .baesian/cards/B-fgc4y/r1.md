proposal: A fixed chance per position
proposal_then: Each held card is drawn with its position's chance, starting at 1 in 10 for "let the agent decide" and 1 in 3 for "check a sample", and a row's first 10 held cards are all drawn. Audits grow with what's held.
alternative: A fixed number of audits a week
alternative_then: About five audits a week across all rows, spread by row, whatever agents file. Your audit load stays flat; each card's chance is set from how many were held the week before, and recorded with it.
crux: Should the number of audits you answer grow with how much agents file, or stay the same each week?
crux_proposal: Grow with filing
crux_alternative: Stay the same
crux_preference: true
crux_fact: Bounding a row's miss rate under 10% takes 29 audits with no miss. With a row's first 10 all drawn, that's about 67 held cards at 1 in 3, or 200 at 1 in 10. At five a week over six rows, it's about 35 weeks per row. A fixed chance can be rechecked from the card and the preference file; a weekly budget, like the burn-in, needs the count of earlier held cards.
rationale: A fixed chance is set when the card is filed, from the preference file alone, and audits each row in proportion to what it holds back, which is where misses add up. The values are guesses, kept in the preference file to tune.
wrong_if: Audits arrive faster than you answer them, so many stay unanswered.
check: The report's unanswered audits per week while dogfooding, against the audits drawn.
serves: a miss rate each row can bound, at a cost that follows what's held

P14's decision 10 ([[Routing and Preferences]], section 2, "How a position becomes an outcome"). The preference file holds rate_agent_decides, rate_sample and burn_in ([[Formats for v1]], section 2), and the gate record holds drawn, p and burn_in (section 3). A budget would replace the two rates with a weekly number and a rule for each card's chance. A nonzero chance at the lowest position keeps every held card's miss rate estimable, since every unit needs some chance of selection (ISA 530). The burn-in starts strict and loosens with evidence, as Meta's RADAR did. In a hosted repo the relay draws from its own signature (D158), so under either option the agent can't choose which cards are drawn. Evidence: [[Preference Sliders and the Gate]], sections 1 and 6.
