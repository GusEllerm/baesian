proposal: A passkey signature on hard cards
proposal_then: Answering a hard card asks for your face, fingerprint or PIN, and the passkey signs that answer. baesian verify checks it offline against your public key in the repo; the proof doesn't rest on the service.
alternative: The service's signature only
alternative_then: "Hard cards are answered like the others, signed by the service: one signature format and no keys of yours in the repo. Whoever controls the service could mint an answer to a hard card."
crux: Should proof that a person approved a hard card hold even if the service itself is misused or compromised?
crux_proposal: It should
crux_alternative: The service's word is enough
crux_fact: Hard cards are spending money, security, deleting data and publishing (D42). You run the service for others (D129), so its operator, or anyone who takes it over, could sign any answer. A passkey signature covers the answer's exact bytes and records that the device verified you. It rests on WebAuthn details that are easy to get wrong, and a synced passkey's key is held by Apple or Google.
rationale: For the cards that matter most, proof that doesn't depend on trusting the operator, and the check also stops an agent answering through a signed-in browser.
wrong_if: The extra check makes hard cards slow enough that you put them off, or passkey signing fails on the devices you use.

A WebAuthn assertion is a signature by your passkey over data that includes the server's challenge. If the challenge is a hash of the answer, the passkey signs the answer itself, and anyone with your public key can check it later. W3C's Secure Payment Confirmation uses the same idea for payments. See [[Hosted Service]], section 4, and [[Hosted Service - Strand B5 Prior Art and Security]], F13.
