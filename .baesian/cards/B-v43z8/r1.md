title: What a second person's different answer does
question: When a member answers a card someone else decided, and chooses differently, does someone settle it with both answers kept, or does the latest answer decide?

reverse: moderate
proposal: Someone settles it
proposal_then: nothing changes until the difference is raised. Then the card's reviewer gets the two answers as a card, unnamed until they choose; an owner settles what they can't. The answer not taken stays with the decision, with its reason.
alternative: The latest answer decides
alternative_then: the card's answer becomes the second person's at once, as it would today, and its agent is told the answer changed and who changed it. The earlier answer stays in the record as an earlier answer.
crux: When two people on a project answer the same card differently, should the second answer replace the first?
crux_proposal: "No: someone settles it"
crux_alternative: "Yes: the latest decides"
crux_preference: true
crux_fact: "Today the latest answer on a card decides it, whoever gives it, and the agent gets a brief saying the answer changed, by whom, and which commits were built on the old one. People differ often: on patches scored by more than one reviewer, 15% to 37% had conflicting scores, and the objection usually came second; this project's own blind agreement with its agent was 59% when last measured. Cochrane's rule for two reviewers is to set beforehand how a disagreement is settled, and to keep both original answers. Settling adds a step, and a card, each time two people differ."
rationale: With several people, a changed answer stops being one person changing their mind. Letting the last to look decide makes the outcome depend on who looked last, and sends the agent to rework on a difference nobody has weighed. A named person settling it, with the other view kept, is how every process the research found ends a disagreement.
alternative_rationale: "It's the rule that exists, it needs nothing new, and it trusts the team: whoever answers second has seen the first decision land and still chose otherwise, which is the better-informed view. The objection usually comes second, and is best acted on at once; sending it through another card delays a correction the agent could have made, and adds cards about cards."
serves: a decision changes only when someone answerable for it chooses to change it
