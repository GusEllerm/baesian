title: Which cards take two answers
question: Should a card take two members' answers, each blind to the other, only on the kinds of decision a team marks, or on every hard card?
doing: drafting the proposal for several reviewers per project

reverse: cheap
proposal: Only the kinds a team marks
proposal_then: one answer decides a card unless its kind is marked. Nothing is marked to start; a team marks, say, security or data loss, and those cards need two members who agree, or a third to settle it. It's built last.
alternative: Every hard card
alternative_then: "every hard card needs two members' answers once a project has two members: security, privacy, money, data loss, publishing, and whatever a repo's settings raise to hard. The agent waits for both, and for a third when they differ."
also_considered:
  - Two answers on every card
  - A second answer on a card Baesian picks at random, as an audit
crux: Is one person's answer enough on a hard card, unless a team says otherwise?
crux_proposal: Yes, unless a team marks the kind
crux_alternative: "No: hard cards take two"
crux_preference: true
crux_fact: In systematic reviews, one screener missed about 1 relevant study in 10 and two independent screeners about 3 in 100, with more false alarms and more time. Reading mammograms twice helped in older studies and showed no significant gain in newer ones; reviewers of radiology suggest it only for high-risk cases. A second check that sees the first's answer, or the same automated recommendation, made no measurable difference; Baesian's would be blind to both. An agent can't proceed on a hard card until it's answered. In this repo, product behaviour and privacy are raised to hard, so both of today's cards were hard. On a project of two, 'every hard card' means both people answer each one.
rationale: The evidence favours doubling by risk, not everywhere, and a team knows which of its misses are costly better than a default does. 'Hard' already means the agent stops; making every such stop wait for two people slows the cards that block the most, and on a project of two it makes each one both people's job.
alternative_rationale: "Hard cards are the ones that can't be undone, which is exactly where a second independent answer is shown to catch what one person misses, and where one account acting alone is a risk worth a control. Left as something to switch on, it won't be: nobody marks a kind until after the miss. Two on hard cards is the safe default, and a team that finds it slow can unmark a kind."
serves: a second answer is spent where a team has decided a miss costs most
