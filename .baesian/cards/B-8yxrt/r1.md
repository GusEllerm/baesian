proposal: Loosening takes your passkey
proposal_then: A change that moves any row toward held, or turns enforcing on, asks for your passkey and keeps its proof beside the file. verify checks it as it checks a hard-card answer (D139). Tightening needs no prompt.
alternative: The service's signature only
alternative_then: Every preference change is signed by the service, like any answer (D147), with no passkey prompt when you move a slider. An agent driving your signed-in browser could loosen a row.
crux: Is letting cards past you as consequential as answering a hard card?
crux_proposal: If it is
crux_alternative: If it isn't
crux_fact: A held card counts as settled at merge, with no answer (D150). Today only hard-card answers take the passkey (D139). P15 notes that an agent driving a signed-in browser could answer provisional and blocking cards.
rationale: One loosened row can settle many future cards without you, so it's at least as consequential as one hard answer. Tightening can't hide anything, so it stays free.
wrong_if: You change sliders often enough that the passkey prompt becomes a reason not to tune them.

D147 has the service sign preference files in a hosted repo. D139 adds the passkey only for hard-card answers. Since D150, a loosened row settles cards without an answer, which is what this card weighs. The preference file and the proof file are in [[Formats for v1]], sections 2 and 7.
