P15 left data residency open, and its section 8 said "an object's location is set by a hint (jurisdiction controls weren't checked)". They now are.

**What Cloudflare offers** (docs read 2026-11-03):
- *Durable Objects:* "Jurisdictions are used to create Durable Objects that only run and store data within a region". The jurisdictions are `eu`, `us` (added 2026-06-26) and `fedramp`. A jurisdiction is set through the namespace when an object's ID is made, and "the same name" gives "different IDs in different jurisdictions". "Workers may still access Durable Objects constrained to a jurisdiction from anywhere in the world", and "A DurableObjectId will be logged outside of the specified jurisdiction for billing and debugging purposes."
- *D1 and KV* take a jurisdiction too, set only when a database or namespace is created (D1 since 2025-11-05; KV generally available 2026-10-02). KV data "can be cached outside the jurisdiction".
- *Regional Services* controls where requests are handled. It wasn't checked whether it's available on Workers Paid.

**What the law asks** (not legal advice; the agent isn't a lawyer):
- *Chapter V:* GDPR's Article 44 makes transfers to a third country lawful under the conditions of Chapter V, such as an adequacy decision or standard contractual clauses. It doesn't require storage in the EU.
- *Direct sign-ups:* EDPB Guidelines 05/2021 (version 2.0, adopted 14 February 2023), Example 1: a person in Italy giving data to a company outside the EU "does not constitute a transfer". Example 2: when that company then uses a processor outside the EU, "such disclosure ... would amount to a transfer". The second is Baesian's case with Cloudflare.
- *Cloudflare's DPA* (version 6.4, 3 April 2026, from search results): Cloudflare is certified under the Data Privacy Framework, transfers under it aren't restricted transfers, and the standard clauses apply otherwise. Cloudflare's GDPR page says the DPA is part of its self-serve agreement.
- *EU organisations as controllers:* where an organisation's repos are on the service, it is the controller and the service its processor (B5 F20), and its transfer to the service needs a mechanism. Unless the operator is certified under the Framework, that means standard clauses in the service's DPA (the agent's reading).
- *The Framework's standing:* the General Court upheld it in Latombe (September 2025), the appeal is C-703/25 P, and Trump v. Slaughter (29 June 2026) held that FTC commissioners are removable at will, which the adequacy decision partly relied on (Skadden, 28 July 2026). It remains valid until a court or the Commission says otherwise.

**Where account-level data lives** isn't decided anywhere yet: email addresses, handles, passkey public keys, the Globus link, the recovery code's hash, machine grants with their hostnames, push subscriptions, and the directory that maps project IDs to objects. Under both options it is one store in the default location; which store (a D1 database, or an object per account) is a storage-layout choice for its own card. Under the second option, a project's place must be known before its ID can be turned into an object, so it's recorded in that directory, or in the project ID itself.

**Not known:** whether you'll run the service personally or through the University, which decides who the controller is and whether Framework certification is open to it.
