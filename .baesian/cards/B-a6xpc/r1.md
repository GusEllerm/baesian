proposal: One tap signs a batch
proposal_then: A hard answer waits on the desk, marked as waiting for your passkey, while you go on to the next card. When no hard card is left in front of you, or you choose to, one tap signs every waiting answer together, and each answer's proof names that signature. `baesian verify` still checks offline that your passkey signed each answer (D139). Agents waiting on those cards hear once you tap.
alternative: One tap opens a window
alternative_then: After a tap, hard answers for the next few minutes need none; the service records that your passkey was checked when the window opened. Nothing waits. CI can check only that the service says a window was open, not that your passkey covered the answer.
crux: Must each hard answer carry your passkey's own signature, checkable without trusting the service?
crux_proposal: Yes, as D139 decided
crux_alternative: "No: a recent passkey check at the service is enough"
crux_fact: D139 put the passkey's signature on each hard answer so the proof doesn't rest on the service, and to tell your answer from an agent's at your keyboard. Within a window, anything driving your browser, a script or an agent using browser tools, could answer hard cards without a tap. The prompt already takes several signatures for one answer ('Step 1 of 2').
rationale: A batch keeps what D139 bought at one tap per sitting rather than per card, and asks nothing of the service's sessions or clock. Its cost is that answers wait for the tap, about a minute over a deck.
wrong_if: Hard cards come one at a time through the day, so a batch is nearly always one answer.

Asked on 2026-11-05, after the setup deck's two hard cards each took a passkey prompt (and, in the installed app, a phone scan each).

**The proposal in more detail.** The passkey's challenge becomes a digest over the list of waiting answers' digests; the proof file beside each answer gives the list and the one assertion. A new form of the passkey proof, so a format change before v1. The desk shows "2 answers waiting for your passkey" with a button, and asks by itself when the last hard card in view is answered.
