title: Whether a repo's preferences have a release stage
question: Should the preferences carry a release stage that moves several rows together when you ship, or do you change presets or rows yourself?

proposal: No stage in v1
proposal_then: Shipping changes nothing by itself. To be asked more about public interfaces and data formats once you have users, you switch preset or move those rows; each change is a new preference file.
alternative: A release stage in the preferences
alternative_then: "The preference file gains a stage: prototype, pre-release or released. Moving to released raises chosen rows, such as public interfaces and data formats, one stop over your own settings, in one change."
crux: When you ship, would you rather flip one setting than move the rows that matter yourself?
crux_proposal: I'd move them myself
crux_alternative: One setting, please
crux_preference: true
crux_fact: Practitioners treat interfaces and schemas as costlier to change once others depend on them (Hyrum's law; Google's reviews differ at alpha, beta and GA). A preference file holds every row (P16), so switching preset replaces rows you moved by hand. No study tests a stage setting.
rationale: A stage is a second dial over the rows, one more thing to explain and to record in each gate record; moving two or three rows when you ship is a small cost, and a stage can be added later.

The second half of P14's decision 17 ([[Routing and Preferences]], section 5, "Not needed before v1"). The research proposes a repo-level stage that moves several rows together, from Fowler, Hyrum's law and Google's release stages ([[Preference Sliders and the Gate]], section 3, "Modifiers, not axes", and section 8, decision 12). The first half, a novelty fact, isn't carded: D145 has v1's gate route on each card's stated facts only, so that the CLI, the relay and CI reach the same answer, and novelty would be derived from a record that differs between them.
