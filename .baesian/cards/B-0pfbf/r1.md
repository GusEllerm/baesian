question: Should the routing gate hold some filed cards back from you, or only order and batch your queue?

proposal: Hold some back, after a shadow period
proposal_then: Cards below a row's setting skip your queue; the agent proceeds, and a random sample comes back as audits. A shadow period first shows what would have been held, with every card still shown.
alternative: Sort and batch only
alternative_then: Every filed card reaches you. Sliders set only order, batching and notifications. Nothing is missed and no audits are needed; the queue stays as long as filing makes it.
crux: Will you accept that a few decisions you'd have overturned never reach you, if that rate is measured and shown?
crux_proposal: Yes, if it's measured
crux_alternative: No, I want to see every one
crux_preference: true
crux_fact: People accept 93% of agent permission prompts, and alert acceptance falls with each extra reminder. A gate's worst misses go unseen, and bounding one kind's miss rate below 10% takes 29 clean audits.
rationale: Reviewing everything alike spends attention without buying safety, and the shadow period gives the real miss rate before anything is held.
serves: a shorter queue, with what it hides measured

P14 proposes per-repo sliders whose two lowest positions hold cards back: the agent proceeds, the card is recorded with a new held status, and a random sample returns as audit cards. The strongest alternative is B4's design F, where nothing is ever hidden. Evidence: [[Preference Sliders and the Gate]], sections 1 and 5. Design: [[Routing and Preferences]], section 2.
