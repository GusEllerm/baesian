proposal: Readable, deleted after delivery
proposal_then: TLS and encryption at rest, a secret scan before upload, and card text deleted after it's answered. The gate can run on the service, and notifications and support can show text. An encryption field is reserved.
alternative: End-to-end encrypted
alternative_then: Only your devices hold the key, wrapped by your passkeys; the service sees sizes, timing and IDs. The gate runs on the agent's machine, notifications carry no text, and losing every passkey loses relayed cards.
crux: Will any feature need to read cards on the server, the classification gate included?
crux_proposal: Yes, or probably
crux_alternative: No
crux_fact: The repo's record keeps every decision either way (D127); the relay holds only what's waiting. A gate run on the service is out of the agent's reach; run on the agent's machine, it's beside the agent. Encryption keys in a browser come from passkeys (WebAuthn PRF), which work with iCloud Keychain in Safari 18+ and Chrome on Android, but not Windows Hello. This is the same question as decision 4 in the sliders synthesis.
rationale: The gate's safest home is where cards are relayed (the sliders note, B4 I4), and deleting text after delivery shrinks what a breach exposes to what's waiting. Reserving the field keeps encryption open without a migration.
wrong_if: The gate is settled to run on the agent's machine and nothing else needs to read cards on the server, or early users say they won't put private-repo cards on a readable server.

The relay passes cards up from the agent's machine and answers down to it (D127). Whether it can read what it passes decides where the classification gate can run (D131), what a notification can say, and how much a breach of the service would expose. Strand B5 found end-to-end encryption workable but costly: key recovery, uneven passkey support, and no server-side features. See [[Hosted Service]], section 1, and [[Hosted Service - Strand B5 Prior Art and Security]], F4 and R1.
