title: Where verify finds the reviewer's passkey keys
question: Should baesian verify trust the reviewer's passkey keys from CI's protected settings, or from the record, with each new key vouched for by a trusted one?

reverse: moderate
proposal: Pinned in CI, beside the service key
proposal_then: Each passkey key you answer hard cards with is listed in CI's protected settings, as the service's key is (D142), with a copy in the record. Adding a new passkey means adding it there by hand.
alternative: In the record, each new key vouched for
alternative_then: Your first key is trusted once it's committed. Each later key needs a proof from a key already trusted, so the service can't add one alone. Losing every passkey means resetting the keys by hand.
crux: Will you add new passkeys often enough that editing CI's settings each time is a burden?
crux_proposal: If rarely
crux_alternative: If often
crux_fact: A synced passkey (iCloud Keychain, Google Password Manager) is one key across your devices; a security key or another platform's passkey is a new one. A commit can't change CI's protected settings, but it can add files to the record.
rationale: It's the strongest anchor, the one D142 chose for the service's key, so a hard-card proof holds even against the service's operator (D139). With synced passkeys, most people would add a key rarely.
wrong_if: You add or replace passkeys more than a few times a year, or a second reviewer joins and needs CI access to enrol.
check: Count passkey enrolments per account on the service over the first months of dogfooding.

D139 has the passkey sign hard-card answers, checked offline against your public key in the repo, so that the proof doesn't rest on the service. Both options keep a copy of the key in the record. They differ in what verify trusts when a key appears there. The key and proof files are in [[Formats for v1]], section 7.
