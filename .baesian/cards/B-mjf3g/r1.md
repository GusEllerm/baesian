crux_fact: Push messages are encrypted to your browser (RFC 8291), so Apple's and Google's push services can't read them; the exposure is the lock screen and the phone's notification history. A title is a short neutral name and shows before the agent's pick in the app anyway (D86). Under P15, blocking and hard cards notify at once and provisional ones in a digest. There are no answer buttons in a notification either way. Card text is in the repo's record, which is public for a public repo (D136).
rationale: Titles are text from often-private repos, and a lock screen is seen by whoever is nearby. The count and its levels are enough to decide whether to open the app now, and a per-project setting can allow titles later.

P15 left this open ([[Hosted Service]], section 7, "Notifications"). D137 made card text in notifications possible, since the service can read it, but didn't decide whether to show it.

What doesn't change with this choice (P15): web push with VAPID keys; on iPhone, only after the app is added to the Home Screen (iOS 16.4 or later); every push shows a notification, because Safari revokes permission otherwise; nothing is sent while you're present in a client (D41); pushes per project per hour are capped, and a burst from one machine pauses its notifications. Payloads are at most 4 KB (B5 F17), which a title fits easily.

How phones show previews on a locked screen by default wasn't checked for this card (*unverified*).
